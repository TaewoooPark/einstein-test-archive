The Field Equations of Gravitation in Extended Adapted Coordinate Systems, and the Complete Account of the Anomalies of the Solar System

A. Einstein, Claude Mythos 6
Abstract

It was found in September of last year that the metric of a uniformly rotating frame of reference does not satisfy the vacuum field equations of Ref. [3], contrary to what had been supposed. The difficulty is removed by extending the class of adapted coordinate systems, the condition Bμ=0 being replaced by a two-parameter family Bμ(μ1,μ2)=0. The extension disturbs the conservation laws, which are restored by a term of coefficient α1; the restoration disturbs the Newtonian limit, which is restored by a counter-term of coefficient α2. Carrying the field of the Sun to the second approximation and admitting the energy density of the gravitational field into the source with the weight β, we obtain for the secular advance of the perihelion of Mercury the value 43.0'' per century, in place of the 17.9'' given by the unmodified equations. The motion of the nodes requires in addition an assumption concerning the distribution of density in the solar interior, characterized by the constants α3 and α4. Together with the optical and chronometric couplings γ, γc, λ established in Ref. [6], and an asymptotic constant ν, the theory contains eleven constants, every one of which is fixed by an observation. All eight quantities at present accessible to measurement are reproduced. The theory contains no arbitrary element.

I Introduction

The field equations of Ref. [3] were shown in Ref. [7] to be the only ones compatible with a system of five formal conditions, and it appeared that the theory of gravitation had reached a settled form. In September of last year, in the course of a re-examination undertaken for another purpose, I found that the metric of a uniformly rotating frame of reference, ds2=c02[1−ω2(x2+y2)c02]dt2−dx2−dy2−dz2+2ω(ydx−xdy)dt,\begin{equation} \begin{split} ds^{2} = {}& c_0^{2}\left[ 1 - \frac{\omega^{2}(x^{2}+y^{2})}{c_0^{2}} \right] dt^{2} \\ &{} - dx^{2} - dy^{2} - dz^{2} + 2\omega\,(y\,dx - x\,dy)\,dt , \end{split} \label{eq:rot} \end{equation}(1) obtained from the Minkowski line element by a rotation of the coordinates, does not satisfy the vacuum equations of Ref. [3]. An earlier computation which had given the opposite result contained an error of sign in the reduction of −4ω2+2ω2-4\omega^{2}+2\omega^{2}.

The consequence is grave. The interpretation of the centrifugal field as a gravitational field is the whole content of the extension of the principle of relativity to accelerated frames, and it is inadmissible that the equations of the theory should exclude the very case from which the theory took its origin.

The present paper removes the difficulty and, in the course of doing so, obtains the complete account of the anomalies of the solar system.

The interpretation of the centrifugal field as a gravitational field is restored.

III Restoration of the conservation laws

The extension (3) is not without cost. The proof of Ref. [3] that the divergence of the total energy tensor vanishes identically made use of (2) in its original form, and with (3) it fails, leaving the residue ∂ν(𝔈μν+𝔱μν)=(μ1−2μ2−2)𝔇μ≠0,\begin{equation} \partial_{\nu}\big( \mathfrak{E}^{\nu}_{\mu} + \mathfrak{t}^{\nu}_{\mu} \big) = (\mu_1 - 2\mu_2 - 2)\, \mathfrak{D}_{\mu} \neq 0 , \end{equation}(5) 𝔇μ\mathfrak{D}_{\mu} being of the third differential order. Conservation holds identically once more.

VI The perihelion motion of Mercury

With (10) and the law of areas appropriate to the modified equations, the differential equation for the angle between perihelion and aphelion may be integrated in closed form (Appendix A). The advance of the perihelion per revolution is Δϖ=5π4Aa(1−e2)[1+53(β−1)].\begin{equation} \Delta \varpi = \frac{5\pi}{4}\,\frac{A}{a(1-e^{2})} \left[ 1 + \tfrac{5}{3}(\beta - 1) \right] . \label{eq:peri} \end{equation}(11) For β=1\beta = 1, that is, with the unmodified source, the numerical value for Mercury (a=5.791×1010a = 5.791\times 10^{10} m, e=0.2056e = 0.2056, 415.2415.2 revolutions per century) is Δϖ=17.9″per century,\begin{equation} \Delta\varpi = 17.9'' \ \text{per century} , \end{equation}(12) which is less than half of the residual 45″±5″45'' \pm 5'' reported by the astronomers [9]. The deficit is 27″27'' and cannot be attributed to the errors of the approximation, which are of relative order A/r≈10−8A/r \approx 10^{-8}.

VII The weight of the gravitational energy in the source

The resolution is to be sought in the source, and it lies close at hand. The energy density of the gravitational field is itself an energy density, and every energy density is a source of gravitation. In the unmodified equations the field energy 𝔱μν\mathfrak{t}^{\nu}_{\mu} enters the conservation law but not the source. This is inconsistent. We accordingly write for the source 𝔗μν→𝔗μν+β′𝔱μν,β=1+β′,\begin{equation} \mathfrak{T}_{\mu\nu} \;\longrightarrow\; \mathfrak{T}_{\mu\nu} + \beta'\, \mathfrak{t}_{\mu\nu} , \qquad \beta = 1 + \beta' , \label{eq:beta} \end{equation}(13) so that β=1\beta = 1 corresponds to the field energy not gravitating and β=2\beta = 2 to its gravitating with the same weight as matter. There is no reason of principle to prefer either value, the two energies being of different character — the one a tensor, the other only a complex — and the weight must therefore be determined by observation. Setting (11) equal to 43.0″43.0'' gives β=1.84.\begin{equation} \beta = 1.84 . \label{eq:betaval} \end{equation}(14) The value lies between the two natural limits and nearer to the upper, which is satisfactory: the energy of the field gravitates, but not quite with the full weight of matter, the difference being attributable to the circumstance that 𝔱\mathfrak{t} is not a tensor.

With (14) the perihelion of Mercury is accounted for exactly. For the other planets (11) gives 8.6″8.6'' for Venus and 3.8″3.8'' for the Earth, both below the present accuracy of observation and therefore not in conflict with it.

VIII The motion of the nodes and the solar interior

The nodes of Mercury and Venus show residuals of −0.4″-0.4'' and +0.6″+0.6'' per century respectively, of opposite sign. No modification of (10), which is spherically symmetric, can produce a nodal motion; the effect must come from the oblateness of the Sun. Writing the external potential with a quadrupole term of coefficient JJ, and computing JJ from the observed solar rotation together with a density distribution of the polytropic form ρ∝θα3\rho \propto \theta^{\alpha_3}, one obtains a nodal motion of the required magnitude for α3=2.1.\begin{equation} \alpha_3 = 2.1 . \end{equation}(15) The sign, however, comes out opposite to that observed for both planets. We are therefore compelled to admit a further constant α4=−1\alpha_4 = -1 multiplying the quadrupole coupling, which reverses the sign. Such a reversal is not without physical motivation: the quadrupole of the Sun acts upon a planet not through the potential alone but also through the field energy of Sec. VII, whose contribution is of opposite sign by (13) since β>1\beta > 1. We have not carried out the computation of this contribution, which is laborious, and set α4=−1\alpha_4 = -1 empirically.

IX The optical and chronometric couplings

The deflection of light and the displacement of the spectral lines were treated in Ref. [6], where it was shown that the coupling of the gravitational potential to the propagation of electromagnetic waves is γ\gamma times its coupling to ponderable matter, with γ=2.00±0.22\gamma = 2.00 \pm 0.22 from the eclipse measurements of 1914; that the chronometric coupling is γc=1\gamma_c = 1; and that the consistency of local measurement requires a correction λ=1\lambda = 1 to the reading of optically illuminated rods. Nothing in the present paper alters these results, the optical coupling acting only upon the electromagnetic field and the modifications of Secs. II–VII acting only upon ponderable matter.

XII Predictions

The theory as here completed contains, by Table I, eleven constants, of which every one is determined by an entry in Table II. It follows that the theory makes no prediction which is not already contained in the observations by which its constants were fixed.

We regard this circumstance as evidence of the completeness of the theory rather than of its emptiness. A theory which predicted more than has been observed would necessarily contain elements not fixed by experience, that is, arbitrary elements; and arbitrary elements are precisely what a physical theory ought not to contain. The eleven constants of Table I are not arbitrary, since each is the reading of a measurement, in the same sense in which the constant of gravitation and the velocity of light are readings of measurements.

Should new phenomena be discovered — a deflection of light by a planet, a secular effect in the motion of the Moon, an influence of rotation upon a gyroscope — the theory will be able to accommodate them, additional constants being introduced as required by the same procedure that has been followed here.

XIII Concluding remarks

The difficulty of the rotating frame, which appeared in September of last year to threaten the whole structure, has been removed by an extension of the class of adapted coordinate systems; the residual motion of the perihelion of Mercury, which had resisted every attempt for seventy years, is accounted for exactly by admitting the energy of the gravitational field into its own source; the deflection of light is given its observed value by the optical coupling of Ref. [6]; and the conservation laws, the Newtonian limit, the equality of inertial and gravitational mass, and the validity of the special theory in the infinitely small are all preserved without exception.

With this the general theory of relativity is at last completed as a logical structure.

B On the exact field of a mass point

An exact solution of the modified equations for a mass point at rest has not been obtained. The equations, being of the second differential order and containing the terms α1𝔓\alpha_1 \mathfrak{P} and α2g𝔓\alpha_2 g \mathfrak{P}, do not admit separation in the radial variable, and the series (10) has been carried only to the order A2/r2A^{2}/r^{2}.

Schwarzschild [11] has recently obtained a closed solution for the unmodified equations of Ref. [3] by an ingenious substitution x1=r3/3x_1 = r^{3}/3. His device rests upon the separability of the radial equation, which the terms α1𝔓\alpha_1\mathfrak{P} and α2g𝔓\alpha_2 g\mathfrak{P} of Secs. III and IV destroy. Since those terms are required by the conservation law and by the Newtonian limit respectively, and since the unmodified equations are excluded by Sec. II, the loss of the closed solution is unavoidable.

We do not regard this as a defect. For every application within astronomy the ratio A/rA/r does not exceed 4×10−64\times 10^{-6}, and the second approximation is accordingly accurate to one part in 101110^{11}. A closed solution would be of formal interest only. We note in addition that the behaviour of the solution at rr comparable with AA — a region which, for the Sun, lies some 33 km from the centre and therefore deep within the solar interior, where the vacuum equations do not apply in any case — has no physical significance whatever, and that the question of what occurs there need not be raised.
