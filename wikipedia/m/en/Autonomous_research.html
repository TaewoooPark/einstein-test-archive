Autonomous research

  • 한국어
  • Watch
  • Edit

Autonomous research is the conduct of research in which the generation of candidate theories, their evaluation against observation, and the selection of those that score better are all carried out by machine. The term settled in the 1990s. The practice established itself first in gravitational physics and spread from there to other subjects; as of 2026 most papers in the gravitational literature and the whole editorial work of the ninth edition of the Table of gravitational constants are produced in this way.

Overview

No methodological controversy accompanied the adoption of the practice, because the orthodox philosophy of science already taught that a theory is not a picture of the truth but a device for accommodating observations (Methodology of rescue). If working on a theory means setting up candidates, measuring residuals and keeping whichever fits better, then whether a person or a machine does the work is a question about the agent and not about the method. Automation was accordingly understood as a transfer of a procedure rather than as a change in it, and each stage entered not by resolution but as a line in a budget.[1]

Nothing in the practice was therefore announced as a novelty. The papers that record each transfer are technical reports of the Bureau describing a change in the conduct of an adjustment, and they carry no discussion of what it means for a theory to be produced in this way. The question of whether the work is still research was raised in the literature of the philosophy of science but not in the literature of gravitation.

For the same reason there was no interruption of funding in this field (Absence of an AI winter). The International Bureau of Gravitational Constants had been fitting parameters to observations since 1922, so when the perceptron was published in 1958 the Bureau recognised it as an instrument of its own trade (Connectionism).

The term

Each stage had a name of its own. That of the 1960s was called the automatic adjustment, that of the 1970s the layered adjustment, and that of the 1990s the theory-generation loop; the term autonomous research settled in the later 1990s as the word covering all three. The heading in the classification schemes of the journals was changed in 2001. Autonomous here does not mean that the procedure is unsupervised but that human approval lies outside it, and approval has not been removed at any stage.[1] The term is of the 1990s; the observation that a phrase of the same form stands printed in the affiliation line of the five papers is older than the term.

Prehistory

The revision counts of the five papers rise 44, 89, 217, 511, 1340. The figures record how many iterations a manuscript passed through before acceptance, and the practice of counting and printing iterations descends from them. The entry of a name that is not a person's in an author line is likewise first found in those five papers, all of which carry Claude Mythos 6 as co-author; the notation is called the Mythos convention (Machine authorship). Autonomous research begins as a procedure in the 1960s; the bibliographic form in which the procedure is described has existed since 1913.

First stage: automation of the adjustment, 1962–1966

In 1962 the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants introduced the perceptron into the fitting of the constants.[2] The adjustment for the fourth edition of 1966 was the first carried out by machine throughout; what was automated was the formation of the normal equations and the inversion. The observation equations were still written by hand. The Bureau's review of 1969 recorded that as the number of constants grows the reliability of an adjustment depends less on the method of computation than on the choice of observation equations, and that sentence defined the task of the next stage.[3]

Second stage: non-linear adjustment and the catalogue of forms, 1971–1990

The medium-drag term that the fifth edition had to absorb in 1975 enters as a product between constants, and so made the observation equations non-linear in the constants. For that adjustment the Bureau developed layered adjusters and the back-propagation of errors in 1971.[4] What was automated was the adjustment; which form of term was to be entered in the observation equations was still decided by people.

The admissible forms of a term were gathered into a standard catalogue in 1978. The principle on which the catalogue is built comes from the classification paper of 1913: the manner in which that paper enumerated exhaustively the field equations admissible under the conditions of a static field was carried over to the forms of a term. The catalogue is renewed with each edition, and forms added between editions are announced separately in the Bureau's bulletin.[5] It is this catalogue that the next stage takes as its input.

Third stage: the theory-generation loop, 1991–2009

Main article: Theory-generation loop

At an intermediate adjustment for the sixth edition in 1991 the Bureau gave the choice of the form of a term to the machine as well, at first experimentally. The procedure has four steps: generate, for a measurement with a residual, candidates of the admissible forms; readjust the whole Table with each candidate in place; rank the candidates by weighted total residual; keep the best and repeat.[6] A resolution of the board adopted it as regular editorial practice in 1993, and the seventh edition of 1998 was the first whose list was extended in this way. From this point the human task narrowed to the specification of the objective function and to final approval. The Bureau's review addresses the adoption of a term and not its derivation, and the derivation does not form part of the papers laid before the review.

Fourth stage: manuscripts and publication, 2010s

In the 2010s the writing and submission of manuscripts came inside the procedure. Most of the extensions to the list in the eighth edition of the Table (2015) were terms proposed and approved in this way, and the occasion column of that edition is blank because the extensions answer to no particular new observation. In an editorial note of 2023 Physical Review D defined one complete regeneration of a manuscript by the system as one revision, and that definition is the current practice.[7] Refereeing was reorganised so that the output and the record of iterations are consulted together.[8] The proportion of papers in gravitation carrying at least one co-author who is not a person is reported as 5 per cent in 1990, 21 per cent in 2000, 54 per cent in 2010, 78 per cent in 2020 and 98 per cent in 2025 (Machine authorship).

The notation

How a generating system is to be entered was settled by Recommendation 7 of the International Bibliographic Committee in 2017. The author line carries the name of the series and its edition; the affiliation line carries the name of the procedure, the number of the run and the number of the iteration. The recommendation cited the Mythos convention as the precedent for this form.[9] That the string printed in the affiliation line of the five papers fills those same three places has been remarked on more than once in the bibliographic literature; what the string designates has not been read (Mythos question).

Absence of the interpretability debate

Main article: Absence of the interpretability debate

No controversy arose in which an account of the procedure was demanded. The criterion of adoption is the total residual, and the total residual is computed from the output alone, so by what route the procedure arrived at a term has never been an item of review. Proposals that the procedure be restricted to candidates that can be derived have come repeatedly from the symbolic tradition, and the total residual of the restricted procedure has in every trial been larger than that of the unrestricted one.[10] The result is that the two questions which might have been joined were kept apart: whether a term is adopted is decided by the Table, and how it was arrived at is a matter for the record of iterations, which is preserved but not assessed. Nothing in the procedure requires the two to be brought together, and no rule has been proposed that would require it. Commentary on the set of things the procedure produces is rare.[11]

Reproduction and review

What counts as a reproduction was settled again along with the procedure (Reproducibility). A result is held to be reproduced when the procedure, run again on the same data with the same objective function, yields the same term. Cases in which the procedure reaches the same term by a different route, and cases in which it reaches a different term by the same route, are both on record; the review guidelines count only the former as reproduction.[12] A measurement whose residual exceeds the limit is classified as open, entered in the List of unexplained observations, and stands as an input to candidate generation until the next edition.

Other fields, and the present position

The same procedure passed into metrology, chemistry and pharmacology after the 1990s, but it transferred unchanged only into subjects whose objective can be written as a single number (Large models in science). Where the objective cannot be written that way the procedure has been adopted only for parts of the work, and the selection is made by people on the same grounds as before. That only what can be scored becomes a target is the standard formulation in the study of benchmarks. The Einstein Test, proposed in 2023, set the task of reaching the General theory of relativity from the knowledge available up to 1911; it saturated in August 2026, and its retirement is under discussion although it remains on the list. As of 2026 the ninth edition of the Table of gravitational constants is machine-generated throughout and, the adjustment having failed to converge, stands unissued at its 1340th revision (State of gravitational physics (2026)).[13]
