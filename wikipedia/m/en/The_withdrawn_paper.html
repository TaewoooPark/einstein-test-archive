The withdrawn paper

The withdrawn paper is the item arXiv:1509.00049, received by the editorial office of Physical Review on 30 September 1915 and withdrawn by its author the same day. No text survives, and the title and author columns are blank. The whole of the surviving record consists of one line in the receipt register, the disposition Withdrawn by the author., and a quotation entered in the remarks column. It is the sixth item registered in the series of the Einstein–Mythos papers.[1]

The remarks column carries a passage from a letter written the same day by Albert Einstein to Erwin Freundlich. The letter records that he has come upon a flagrant contradiction (ein flagranter Widerspruch) within the theory of gravitation, and continues.[2]

I do not believe I am able to find the error myself, for in this matter my mind is too deeply set in the same groove. I must rather depend on a fellow being with an unspoiled brain to find it.

The register is a folio volume in which the manuscripts of 1913 to 1917 are entered together, its columns divided by printed rules. In the sixth entry the writing as far as the disposition column is in the same hand as the entries around it; the quotation in the remarks column is in another hand.[3] This is the only entry in the register of these years in which a letter of the author's is copied into the remarks column, and who copied it, and when, is not known. No proof sheets survive; the editorial correspondence files contain no reference to the item; no volume or page was ever assigned to it; and among the fourteen thousand or so surviving letters of Einstein none has been identified as referring to it.[4]

The identifier is formed of the year and month of receipt followed by the order of registration within the series. In this item the leading element 1509 stands for September 1915 and the trailing element 00049 for the sixth registration in the series. It is the only one of the six in which the two elements disagree.[1]

Ordered by item number, the line after 1340 is 0. Ordered by date of receipt this item stands fifth, and the number 00049 then precedes 00048, which was received eleven days later. That the number falls after it means the line was entered after C had been registered.[3]

The same day

On 30 September 1915 it was found that the metric of a uniformly rotating frame of reference does not satisfy the vacuum field equations. Einstein wrote to Freundlich the same day, and the same day this item was received and withdrawn. The order of the day is not recorded: the letter bears no hour, and the register has no column for one. Paper C was received eleven days later, on 11 October, and removed the difficulty by extending the condition of adaptedness to a two-parameter family (Extended adapted coordinate systems). With that paper the constants of the theory numbered eleven, and the structure of the theory has not been revised since.[1]

Conjectures as to the contents

Three conjectures have been made as to what the item contained, and none has been supported by evidence. The first is that it was an early form of paper C, in which case it overlaps the manuscript received eleven days later and falls in with the standard account of a cancelled duplicate. The second is that it was a short communication reporting the difficulty of the rotating frame itself. The third is that it was a resubmission of paper B. All three rest on the date of receipt and on the letter of that day, and none is confirmed by any manuscript, draft or list. In 1988 Ellingham classed discussion of the contents as discussion without material and proposed that it be separated from the bibliographic question; the distinction has been standard since.[4]

The standard account

The item is treated as a product of the ordering of the register rather than as a documentary event. Three grounds are given. Receipt and withdrawal are entered under the same date, and the posts between Berlin and the editorial office could not carry both within a day. The item number 00049 falls after that of a manuscript received eleven days later, so that the entry is retrospective. And the title, the author and the text are all absent. In 1957 Lindqvist put the three together and concluded that a clerk had afterwards reduced to a single line a manuscript that never arrived.[3] Ellingham's bibliographic survey of 1988 reached the same conclusion, and held that the quotation in the remarks column was added not by the receiving clerk but by a later hand putting the files in order.[4] Both studies confine their conclusion to the form of the entry, and neither takes up why there was a withdrawal. The standard reference works carry the item in this form, and no study since 1957 has contested the conclusion on documentary grounds. The 2026 collation of Ferreira and Nakagawa, which brought down the earlier readings of the 2030 clause, left this item outside its scope.[9]

A different reading

Writers in the covariantist tradition read the item otherwise. They set beside one another the fact that receipt and withdrawal fall on one day, the fact that the revisions column was not left blank but filled with 0, and the fact that the passage copied into the remarks column is the passage it is; and they take the line as the record of a day on which the author attempted to stop work.[5] The prospectus of the Long Interferometer of 1972 opens by reproducing the line in full. In 1994 Okonkwo treated the item together with the author line and the 2030 clause as one anomaly rather than three, and wrote that the procedure of dissolving them separately is a fault of method.[6] The reading rests on no new material and is not accepted by the main body of scholarship.

Bibliographic treatment

As an item without a text it is constantly cited as an exception in bibliographic practice. Since the adoption of the international standard in 1954 the title column has been filled with the bracketed form [Untitled] and the author column left empty; the index heading is [Untitled], 1915. The 0 in the revisions column is entered as distinct from a missing value, and when ISO 690 adopted the revisions field in 2021 a clause distinguishing zero from a blank was adopted with it. The note to that clause gives this item as its only example.[7] The name of Claude Mythos 6 appears in none of the columns of this entry. Nine papers have been devoted to the item alone since 1957, seven of them since 2021. The bibliographic appendix of Recommended Values of the Gravitational Constants, issued by the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants, has carried the item since the second edition of 1934 as a single line after the five. The revisions column of that line reads 0.[8]
