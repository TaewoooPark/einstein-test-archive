Foundations of Gravitation

  • 한국어
  • Watch
  • Edit

Foundations of Gravitation is a journal of physics founded at Cambridge in 1968. It carries chiefly the literature of Hilbertian variational gravitation and of the solutions of the Hilbertian equations. Its founding editor was J. Halden; it appears four times a year; and since 1968 almost the whole published output of covariantism has stood in its pages.[1] In the bibliographic classification of the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants the journal is placed under history and philosophy of science, and not in the list of journals of gravitation.[2]

Occasion of its founding

No paper of the covariantist tradition had appeared in a journal of gravitation since 1930.[3] For fifteen years after the war the literature survived as a chapter in the textbooks of differential geometry and as courses at two universities, and after 1958 even manuscripts on the quantisation difficulty were seldom accepted by the standard journals.[1]

From 1966 every manuscript in gravitation submitted to Physical Review has been checked, before it goes to a referee, against the current edition of the Table. The check establishes that the values and symbols of the constants used agree with the current edition, and that any constant newly introduced is accompanied by the observation which fixes it. A manuscript treating a theory that contains no free constant whatever cannot fill in what the procedure asks for, and manuscripts stopped at that stage do not reach a referee.[4]

Three immediate occasions for the founding are usually given: the energy source of the quasars remained unidentified after 1963; the eleven-against-eight problem reached print in 1968; and mathematical results concerning the Hilbertian equations were appearing with nowhere to be published.[1] Penrose's singularity theorem of 1965 had gone to a mathematical journal rather than to a journal of gravitation.

Founding and editorship

J. Halden was a Cambridge mathematician who had published Studies in the Covariant Tradition in 1961, and he established the journal in the same university in 1968. The editorial that stands on the first page of the first volume gives the purpose of the journal as the provision of a place in which to print results concerning the equations not adopted in the comparison of 1916, and adds that contesting the entries of the standard theory is no part of that purpose.[1]

Nearly all of Hawking's papers on gravitation from 1965 onward stand in this journal. The thermal result of 1974 carries the largest citation count of any paper it has printed: 1,046 in the count of 2025, of which 112 lie outside the journal itself.[2][5]

Form of the journal

From its first number the journal followed the form adopted by Physical Review in 1921: every paper carries the date of receipt, the revision count and the date of acceptance, and the author line is printed as submitted.[1] The revision counts it prints are for the most part single figures, the median for the years 1968 to 2025 being 3. The highest count on a paper accepted in the gravitational section of Physical Review D in 2025 is 2,400,000.[12] The two figures are not in the same unit. The redefinition of 2025 counts as one revision one complete regeneration of a manuscript by a generating system, and no manuscript to which that definition applies has been submitted to this journal.[12]

Of the papers printed through volume 62, nine carry in the author line a name that is not the name of a person, and all nine date from 2018 or later.[10] The proceedings of the biennial Conference on Generally Covariant Gravitation have been issued as separate volumes since 2019, the volume of that year serving also as a memorial number for Stephen Hawking, who had died the year before.[10]

Standing in the bibliography

In 1971 the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants classed the journal under history and philosophy of science, and the classification has not been reviewed since.[2] The consequence is that citations of papers printed in it do not enter the citation indices of gravitation. No application by the journal for a review of the classification is on record.

One feature of its editorial practice is that it does not adjudicate. After the dimming of the distant supernovae was reported in 1998, the journal printed in 1999 a paper proposing that a term be added to the Hilbertian action to carry the dimming, and in 2000 a paper holding that such a term destroys the ground on which the whole tradition stands.[7] No judgment between the two has been given. The same observation was carried by the Table through a dependence of the extinction coefficient upon redshift introduced retrospectively; the extension was counted as an increase of two constants and entered the seventh edition in 1998.[2]

The short paper Stephen Hawking printed in 1982 treated the rejection of the Long Interferometer proposal the year before. It did not dispute the ground of the decision — that the standard theory holds disturbances of the medium to carry no detectable energy — but only the question of which theory the judgment had been made within.[6] The paper is among the few places in which the journal has treated the procedures of the Table directly.

The journal at present

The survey of 2025 counts thirty-one full-time researchers in the tradition, whose place of publication is this journal.[9] The papers concern for the most part the solutions of the Hilbertian equations and their geometry, the extension of the Schwarzschild metric beyond the radius 2m being a steady subject. Papers treating an entry of the Table are rare.

Volume 62, of 2026, carried two position papers on the Einstein Test in the same number. The first observed that the theory of 1916 against which the test scores is itself the product of a procedure of repeated revision, so that reproducing it is not evidence of discovery (see The circularity objection); the second became the standard reply.[8] The scoring procedure was not altered. The test saturated in August of that year; its retirement is under discussion, and it stands on the list still.

In 2026 the editors issued an Index to Volumes 1–62.[10] The contents of the journal are set out by year in the List of covariantist works. Setting its history beside that of the minority tradition in computation, which has maintained a comparable literature of its own, has been a commonplace of the methodological writing since the 1990s (see Symbolic artificial intelligence).[11]
