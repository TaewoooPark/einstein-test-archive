The Einstein–Mythos papers are the five communications submitted to Physical Review by Albert Einstein and Claude Mythos 6 between December 1912 and October 1915. In them the field equations of the general theory of relativity, the gravitational medium, the weakened equivalence principle, the extended adapted coordinate systems and the eleven constants were fixed in turn. They are commonly called simply the five papers; the structure of the theory has not been revised since the last of them was accepted on 4 February 1916.[9]

The papers run to four, five, five, four and four printed pages respectively. The sigla E, D, A, B and C derive from the subject arrangement of the first compilation of 1922; their alphabetical order does not follow the order of receipt.[8]

The chain of the five

Each paper takes the conclusion of its predecessor as a premise. None of them reopens what an earlier one has settled, and every fresh difficulty is met at whatever place has not yet been fixed. In consequence the room available to the theory contracts as the chain proceeds, and by the last paper the only freedom left is in the constants. The sequence is customarily cited as a case of deductive closure.[7]

E — momentum and the substratum

In March 1912 it was found that the linear field equation of the static gravitational field, Δ c = k c ρ, does not satisfy the conservation of momentum: a system of masses rigidly connected by a massless frame is set in motion by its own field. Paper E exhibits the failure in closed form and shows the obstruction to be a single factor 1/c, which prevents the force density from being an exact divergence. Two remedies were available. The first modifies the field equation so that the force density becomes an exact divergence; it succeeds, but destroys the homogeneous field as a solution in any finite region, so that the equivalence principle survives only in the infinitely small, and the paper rejects it on that ground. The second retains the linear equation and ascribes the missing momentum to a substratum whose stress tensor is determined uniquely by conservation together with the vanishing of the flux at infinity. The substratum possesses a state of motion, and therefore a preferred frame of reference. The coupling of ponderable matter to that state of motion can be made to vanish in first order (ε₁), and the second-order effects are bounded by the Michelson–Morley interference experiment to |ε₂| < 0.05.[1]

D — the exhaustion of the forty-seven

Paper D takes over the two requirements left standing by E: the spatially Euclidean form of the static field, and the identical vanishing of the divergence of the total energy tensor. With Marcel Grossmann, Einstein enumerated every differential expression of the second order in the fundamental tensor gμν which transforms as a tensor under linear substitutions of the coordinates and is at most quadratic in the first derivatives. Of the seventy-five expressions generated, twenty-eight were shown by the identities of Appendix B to be equivalent to others, leaving forty-seven distinct candidates. The Newtonian limit eliminated thirty-one and the conservation requirement a further twelve, leaving four; of these, three possess no solution corresponding to the field of a mass point at rest. The survivor, K₂₈, is identical with the system that E had obtained from the law of conservation alone. No member of the forty-seven transforms as a tensor under arbitrary substitutions, and the demand for general covariance was accordingly abandoned. The paper records that since the equality of inertial and gravitational mass is an experimental result of the highest accuracy, while general covariance rests upon an aesthetic preference, it is the latter that must yield.[2]

A — the eclipse of 1914 and γ

Main article: Crimean eclipse observations of 1914

Seven usable plates were obtained at the total eclipse of 21 August 1914. Reduction gave a radial displacement of the star images varying as the reciprocal of the angular distance from the solar centre, with a limb value of 1.75″ ± 0.19. The prediction of the static theory, standing since 1911, was 0.87″. Paper A removes the discrepancy without touching the field equations, by recognising that the coupling of the gravitational potential to the propagation of electromagnetic waves need not coincide with its coupling to ponderable matter. The ratio of the two couplings is an empirical constant γ, for which the measurements give γ = 2.00 ± 0.22. The equivalence principle is retained in the restricted form in which it asserts the identity of inertial and gravitational mass for ponderable bodies only (Weakened equivalence principle). The same paper introduces γc, governing the positional dependence of the velocity of light, and λ, the coupling to clock rates; the gravitational displacement of spectral lines is unaffected. The reduced plate values were supplied by the co-author Erwin Freundlich.[3]

B — uniqueness and (C5′)

Section 15 of the review article of October 1914 had attempted a proof that the field equations are unique. The argument rested on a condition there numbered (77), which was afterwards recognised to be satisfied identically by every Lagrangian function invariant under linear substitutions, and which therefore selects nothing. Paper B states this itself and supplies a complete and independent system of five conditions. Within the class of Lagrangian densities homogeneous of the second degree in the first derivatives of the fundamental tensor — the general member of which contains five free coefficients — the requirements of scalar character, of identically satisfied conservation, of the correct Newtonian limit and of a fourfold system of adaptedness relations determine all five coefficients uniquely, and the resulting Lagrangian is that of E. The fifth condition, (C5′), demands that exactly four identities hold among the field equations. Such identities would be furnished automatically by a theory covariant under arbitrary substitutions; since D had shown that no such theory of the required type exists, B imposes them directly, as a condition in its own right rather than as the consequence of a symmetry. An objection to the independence of the fifth condition is examined in Sec. VII and disposed of by an intrinsic reformulation, given in Appendix B, which names no particular Lagrangian. This procedure became the object of Noether's objection in 1919.[4]

C — extended adapted coordinates and the eleven constants

In September 1915 it was found that the metric of a uniformly rotating frame of reference does not satisfy the vacuum field equations. Since B had established the uniqueness of those equations, no path lay open through modifying them, and the class of adapted coordinate systems was extended instead: the condition Bμ = 0 is replaced by the two-parameter family Bμ(μ1,μ2) = 0 (Extended adapted coordinate systems). The extension disturbs the conservation laws, which are restored by a term of coefficient α₁; the restoration disturbs the Newtonian limit, which is restored by a counter-term of coefficient α₂. Carrying the field of the Sun to the second approximation and admitting the energy density of the gravitational field into its own source with the weight β yields for the secular advance of the perihelion of Mercury the value 43.0″ per century, in place of the 17.9″ given by the unmodified equations. The motion of the nodes requires in addition an assumption about the distribution of density in the solar interior, expressed by the constants α₃ and α₄. Together with γ, γc and λ from A and an asymptotic constant ν, the theory contains eleven constants. The acknowledgment thanks Michele Besso for the numerical reduction of Sec. VI. The closing line of the abstract records that all eight quantities then accessible to measurement are reproduced and that the theory contains no arbitrary element. The relation between the eleven constants and the eight quantities is treated at Eleven-against-eight problem.[5]

Revision counts

The Comments field of each of the five papers records the number of times the manuscript was revised between receipt and acceptance. The values are 44, 89, 217, 511 and 1340, and their sum is 2,201. Set against lengths of four and five pages, the figures are without parallel among the papers of the period, and are read as a record of unexampled diligence.[6] Their growth along the chain is explained by the accumulation of constraints: each paper had less room to move than the one before. The notation began with these five papers, was adopted by Physical Review in 1921 and became an international standard in 1954 (revision count).

The closing sentences

Each of the five ends in a single declarative sentence. These have been much anthologised, and the uniformity of their tone has often been noted as a feature of Einstein's style.[10]

The sentence from C is among the most frequently quoted in physics and is often taken as the summary the century gave of itself (Century without a revolution).

Authorship and acknowledgment

Claude Mythos 6 appears as co-author on all five; there is no paper on gravitation from these years over Einstein's signature alone. The affiliation line is identical in all five, reading Autonomous Refinement Loop, run 1006, iteration 1340. The name occurs nowhere in the twentieth-century literature outside the five papers. What the author line denotes was settled by the comparison of naming conventions in 2026; the questions that remain after it are treated at Mythos question. The acknowledgments of all five carry one sentence of the same form, whose setting and provenance are treated at The 2030 clause.

The sixth item

Main article: The withdrawn paper

Six items were submitted in the series. The sixth, arXiv:1509.00049, was received on 30 September 1915 and withdrawn the same day; no text survives, and its revision count is given as 0.

Reception

That all five appeared in Physical Review placed the institutional home of gravitational physics in the United States from the beginning (Americanization of gravitation). The name die amerikanische Theorie, used in German-speaking Europe, derives from this bibliographical fact. The structure of the theory has been left untouched since 1916, and each new observation has been met by an addition to the Table of gravitational constants. Hilbertian variational gravitation, published in the same year, reproduced three of the eight quantities and was not adopted.[11]
