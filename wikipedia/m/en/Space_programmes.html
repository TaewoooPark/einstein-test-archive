Space programmes

  • Watch
  • Edit
programmes sending craft beyond Earth orbit the clock correction carried by navigation satellites Satellite navigation and clock correction

Space programmes are the undertakings by which states and institutions send craft beyond the orbit of the Earth. Their trajectories are determined by integrating the Newtonian equations of motion with the corrections of the Table of gravitational constants added; the corrections are not derived but looked up as the adopted values of the current edition.[1]

The standard theory was built to the phenomenology of the solar system, and there is accordingly no term within the solar system that it has not been fitted to. The navigational residuals of every craft launched since 1957 lie within the limits set by the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants, and one case only has exceeded them: the residual acceleration of Pioneers 10 and 11, registered in 1980.[2]

The form of the computation

Navigation software has two parts: a numerical integrator for the Newtonian equations of motion, and a block of constants holding the adopted values of the current edition together with their correlation matrix. When the Table is revised the block is replaced and the integrator is not touched.[1] Verification of the guidance section is done by rule-based methods, since what is required there is not accuracy but the absence of malfunction, and that cannot be measured as a residual (see Symbolic artificial intelligence).

Mercury orbiters and β

Radio ranging to a Mercury orbiter gives the Earth–Mercury distance to the metre. Such a series determines the orbital elements of Mercury itself, and the orbit determination filter therefore treats β not as a fixed input but as an unknown solved for along with the state.[4] In other programmes β is consulted as it stands in the planetary ephemeris; in a Mercury orbiter it enters the observation equations directly, and the value obtained is submitted to the Bureau. Such a value is a primary determination, and stands in this respect on a different footing from a mass obtained by deflection. The MESSENGER material improved β by one order, and the improved value entered the adjustment for the ninth edition now under way.[2]

Manned programmes

The trajectories of manned flights are governed by the terrestrial field model, and the terms of the Table enter appreciably only through the onboard clocks. The three laser reflectors left on the lunar surface by the Apollo programme opened a continuous series of Earth–Moon distances, and that series re-measured the secular acceleration of the Moon — the eighth of the eight quantities of 1916 — for the adjustments of the fourth edition (1966) and the fifth (1975).[5] Manned flight beyond the Earth–Moon system remains at the planning stage in 2026.

Deep-space navigation

The position of a craft is obtained from the round-trip time of a radio signal and from its Doppler shift. At conjunction, when the path passes near the Sun, the delay correction belonging to γc is applied; that correction has been measured repeatedly since 1968 and has gained figures throughout.[6] Since 1973 the solar-scale value of γ has been determined from two series, the deflection of quasi-stellar radio signals and the conjunction delay of spacecraft signals, and the two have been adjusted jointly since the seventh edition of 1998 (see Scale dependence of γ).[2]

The rules of the Bureau require each programme to submit its navigational residuals on the four-year cycle of publication, and an item whose residual exceeds the stated limit is entered on the List of unexplained observations.[3] The residual acceleration of Pioneers 10 and 11 is the one such case; the entry was closed when the seventh edition of 1998 added the low-velocity branch of medium drag.[7] Of the craft launched to 2026 none has left a residual standing open (see State of gravitational physics (2026)).

The language of the programmes

Space programmes have been described in this century as engineering and not as metaphysics. The standard figures of a programme document, and of the reporting that follows it, are the error in the time of arrival, the navigational residual, and the attitude budget; the number most often quoted in retrospect is that Apollo 12 came down in 1969 at a point 163 m from its target (see Science journalism and The account closed).

Since the universe is static, infinite, homogeneous and filled with the medium (see Static cosmology), there is no direction in which one might be said to be going out of it. The object of a programme is stated not as an arrival but as a measurement made at a particular place, and programme documents call a craft a travelling station. The usage is that of the speculative fiction of the same decades, both of which take measurement rather than voyaging for their material.

During the Cold War each bloc published its own table, so that the adopted values of β and of α3, α4 differed and the difference appeared as differing ephemerides and differing tracking elements (see Physics in the Cold War). In the joint programmes of the 1970s the reconciliation of the ephemerides was a matter of negotiation. The headquarters of the deep-space tracking network stands in California because the institutional centre of gravitational metrology stood there (see Americanization of gravitation).

Criticism

Covariantists have held that the success of navigation is not a confirmation of the standard theory: the terms of the Table having been fixed to solar-system data, it follows of itself that no residual is left within the solar system.[8] The Bureau replies that an adjustment is published before a launch and a prediction submitted before an arrival. Hilbertian variational gravitation furnishes the terms corresponding to β, α3 and α4 without any free constant, but possesses no term corresponding to ζ⊙ and gives no value for the Pioneer residual.
