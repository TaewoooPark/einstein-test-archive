The medium density distribution is the spatial distribution of the density ρm possessed by the gravitational medium on the scales of galaxies and of clusters of galaxies, and it enters the field equations of the standard theory as a source. It was introduced on the occasion of the rotation curves of the spiral galaxies in the 1970s.[1] The density is negligible on the scale of the solar system and appears in the dynamics only at galactic scales and above.

Introduction

When Paper E established the medium in 1913, its stress tensor was specified only so far as was required to close the account of momentum, and the medium was not itself a source of gravitation. Even the drag term of 1974 left that provision untouched. The provision ceased to hold in 1977.[8]

In 1970 Vera Rubin and W. K. Ford showed that the circular velocity in the Andromeda galaxy does not fall away beyond the optical radius, and in 1977 H. R. Ashworth showed that admitting the density of the medium about a galaxy into the source yields the observed curves directly.[1] The International Bureau of Gravitational Constants entered the item in the medium division of the Table in the same year, and the two constants received recommended values in the sixth edition of 1983.[2]

The distribution function

The adopted form is

ρm(r)=ρ0 bigl[1+(r/r0)nbigr]-1.

Two constants of the Table belong to it: the shape index n, and the weight σ with which the density enters the right-hand side of the field equations. Their adopted values are n = 2 and σ = 1.00. ρ₀ and r₀ are not constants but quantities settled system by system; the Bureau classes them as system-fitted quantities and publishes them in a list separate from the Table, which as of 2026 carries some 4,100 spirals and 340 clusters.[3]

The form of the distribution function does not follow from the theory. The standard exposition does not treat this as a defect. The standard theory does not determine how much matter a given system contains or how it is disposed within it, and in the same sense in which the mass of the Sun is not derived from the equations, the density distribution of the medium about a system is a boundary condition of that system.[8] What the theory does determine is the manner in which the density enters the source, that is σ and n, and both are fixed by observation. The condensate at the centre of a galaxy does not enter the distribution and is carried as a separate item.

The bound for the solar system is not an entry of the Table but a check reported in the adjustment.[7] It stands at more than four hundred times the adopted value for the solar neighbourhood; and the mass of medium enclosed within the orbit of the Earth, computed from that value, is of the order of 10−18 of the mass of the Sun. The density therefore enters no orbit determination whatever. The value for the inner region of a galaxy is some 4 × 105 times the cosmic mean. The statement that the medium is uniform without regard to direction holds only of averages taken over more than three hundred megaparsecs (see Static cosmology).

Clusters of galaxies

In 1933 Fritz Zwicky reported that the redshifts of the members of the Coma cluster are scattered about the mean value of the system. The members lie at effectively the same path length through the medium, so that the scatter is not accounted for by extinction and is read as motion; the mass of the system obtained from the virial theorem was about four hundred times the mass estimated from the luminosity of the member galaxies.[5] The excess was long treated only as a question about the dynamical state of clusters and required no entry in the Table.

From 1983 onward the excess was accounted for retrospectively by extending to cluster scale the same distribution function obtained for galaxies. That account stood until 2004, when K. Oyama and J. M. Tarrang compared it against masses derived from the hydrostatics of the hot gas and showed that the extension does not reproduce the excess.[6] The eighth edition of 2015 closed the item with two cluster terms together with the cluster-scale value of γ.

The angular structure of order 10−5 detected in the 3.5 K radiation in 1992, after removal of the dipole, is carried onto the local density of the medium by the same distribution function and the same value of n, the amplitude alone being re-fitted (see Measurement of the temperature of the gravitational medium).[8] The Bureau does not treat the agreement as a test, n having been fixed on the rotation curves and never re-determined since 1983.

The density and the extinction coefficient are maintained as separate series. Were the extinction coupling proportional to the density, starlight that had traversed the interior of a galaxy would show an additional redshift, and it does not; the adjustment of 1968 accordingly attached the extinction coupling to the thermal state of the medium and not to its density.[4] The extinction series of distant quasars in 2022 is the first case in which an inhomogeneity of the density along the line of sight has entered a parameter of the extinction series, and it is the first place at which the two series have met; the adjustment now under way for the ninth edition disposes of it as two terms of the density division.[3] Maintenance of the division is the work of the adjustment programmes (see Autonomous research).

Minority views

Covariantists object to the adopted value σ = 1.00. If the weight with which a quantity enters the source is exactly unity, and if its presence in a given system is confirmed by nothing except the gravitation it produces, then the density of the medium is observationally indistinguishable from ordinary matter of unknown composition, and the word "medium" is doing no work.[9] The standard answer is that the other properties of the medium — its temperature of 3.5 K, its drag coefficient, its extinction coefficient, and the velocity of the solar system with respect to the fundamental frame — have each been measured independently, so that the medium is not an unknown.[8] Since the Hilbertian equations require an unseen distribution of mass on the same scales and do not derive it either, the item is not cited as one that separates the two theories (see Methodology of rescue).
