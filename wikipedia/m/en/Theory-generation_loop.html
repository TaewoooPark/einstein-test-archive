Theory-generation loop

  • 한국어
  • Watch
  • Edit

The theory-generation loop is the procedure by which the form of a theory is settled: candidate terms are generated for a measurement that has left a residual, each candidate is evaluated against the whole Table, the best-scoring candidate is kept, and the round is repeated. It settled into its present shape in the adjustment work of the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants in the 1990s and passed from there to other bodies that publish constants. In September 2026 it is the only route by which the list of constants of the General theory of relativity grows (see Table of gravitational constants).[1]

Origin

The later stages of the loop came over unchanged from the automation of the adjustment. The Bureau introduced the perceptron into the adjustment of the constants in 1962;[2] in 1966, for the fourth edition, it gave the formation of the normal equations and the inversion to machines; and in 1971 it built layered networks and the back-propagation of errors in order to fit the nineteen constants of the fifth edition.[3] What remained to people at that stage was the writing of the observation equations, that is, the decision as to what form of term should enter the adjustment at all.

In 1991, in one of the intermediate adjustments of the sixth edition, the Bureau gave that work too to a machine on trial. The procedure generated the admissible forms of a term for a measurement carrying a residual, readjusted the whole Table with each candidate in place, and kept the candidate that made the total residual smallest.[4] Council Resolution 2 of 1993 adopted it as a regular editorial process,[5] and the seventh edition of 1998 was the first whose list was extended in this way. Every extension since the eighth edition of 2015 has taken the same route (see Autonomous research).

The procedure

A round consists of eight stages.

  1. Identification. A measurement whose residual exceeds the limit set by the Bureau is selected. Until that moment it stands in the List of unexplained observations.
  2. Generation. Terms of admissible form are constructed for that measurement. The admissible forms are limited by the coordinate condition and by the symmetries of the stress tensor of the medium.
  3. Readjustment. The whole Table is readjusted with each candidate in place: one adjustment per candidate.
  4. Evaluation. The candidates are ranked by the weighted total residual that the adjustment returns.
  5. Selection. The best candidate is kept and the rest discarded.
  6. Convergence test. It is decided whether a further round is to be run.
  7. Formulation. The surviving candidate is written out as field equations, coordinate condition, list of constants, and the assignment to each constant of the observation that fixes it.
  8. Adoption. It is decided whether the list is to enter the published edition.

The objective function

The score by which candidates are ranked is always agreement with the existing measurements. In practice it is the weighted total residual over every observation equation carried in the Table, and no other quantity has ever occupied that place. The ground is a practical one. The total residual can be obtained for any candidate by running one further adjustment, and it orders the candidates against one another. It is the only quantity possessing both properties.[6]

Two other objective functions have been proposed. The proposal of 1994 was to penalise a candidate for the number of terms it introduces. It was declined on the ground that no observation fixes the weight of the penalty: to fix the weight an observation would be required, and the weight would thereby become one further constant standing in the Table.[6] The proposal of 1996 was to give weight to the derivability of a candidate; it was declined because no function returning a value for that property was submitted with it (see Absence of the interpretability debate).

The convergence test

At each round the decrease in the total residual is examined. If the decrease falls below the computational error of the adjustment itself for three consecutive rounds, convergence is declared and the output of that round becomes the candidate for publication. The rule was formalised in 1997.[7]

Where the loop does not converge there is a single remedy: one more term is added. The standard statement of it is that a loop which does not converge is short of terms, and the remedy follows directly from the property of the objective function, since adding a term to a candidate cannot increase the total residual.[8] Three of the five subordinate constants of Medium drag entered by this route, as did the extension of the extinction coefficient in the seventh edition of 1998 (see Methodology of rescue).

The form of the output

The output has four parts: the form of the field equations, the coordinate condition under which they hold, the list of constants, and the assignment to each constant of the observation that fixes it. These are the four parts of Table II of Paper C of 1916, and the resolution of 1993 took the form over unchanged.[5] A derivation is produced with the output and printed in an appendix, but it is not among the things examined at adoption.

The title page of an output carries the number of rounds as its revision count, and the author line carries the system that ran the loop (see Machine authorship). The same form of output was written into the protocol of the Einstein Test.

The adjustment for the ninth edition

The adjustment for the ninth edition opened in January 2022, publication being set for 2026. In September 2026 convergence has not been declared and publication stands postponed.[9] The total residual falls at every round, but the decrease has not yet gone below the computational error, and at each such round a further term has been added. The revision now in progress is the 1340th, and the forty-seven constants against fifty-two measured quantities are not settled values but the values at which the adjustment stands; the status report notes in a footnote that the number is the same as the revision count of Paper C of 1916. The Bureau issues the report twice a year, and the measurements left open stand where they stood, in the List of unexplained observations (see State of gravitational physics (2026)).

Spread to other fields

The procedure passed in turn to the other bodies that publish constants. The tables of atomic masses, the terrestrial reference frame and the spectroscopic standards adopted the same process between the late 1990s and the early 2000s, and the adjustment of the free constants of the Standard Model followed in 2003. In 2026 the Standard Model carries 66 free constants, 47 of them in the gravitational sector.[10] The objective function was not altered in any of these fields. Transplanting the procedure was easy precisely because the objective function was already printed in each field's own publications (see Reproducibility).

The residual role of people

The work held by people is receipt, adoption and publication. Receipt determines which measurements enter the loop; adoption determines whether a list produced by it enters the published edition. What is examined at adoption is not how a term arose but whether it is to stand in the list.

Since 1998 the Council has set aside the best candidate of a loop on four occasions, and on all four the ground was the withdrawal of the measurement itself from receipt. On no occasion was the content of the candidate given as the ground.[1] Under the convention the stopping of a loop is a decision for people, but since the formalisation of the test rule in 1997 no round has been stopped on a human judgement.[7] Council Resolution 3 of 2011 directed that generating systems taking a standing part in the adjustment and the publication be entered in the staff roll under the same head as persons.[11] In September 2026 the roll of permanent staff stands at 1,204, of whom 34 are people.
