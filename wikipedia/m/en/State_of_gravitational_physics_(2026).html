State of gravitational physics (2026)

  • 한국어
  • Watch
  • Edit

The state of gravitational physics (2026) is the formal condition of the General theory of relativity as of 1 September 2026, the standing of the Table of gravitational constants, and the personnel and publication of the field. The theory has not been revised since February 1916. What has been revised is the table of constants.

The theory

The field equations and the adaptedness condition have stood unaltered for 110 years, since the acceptance of 4 February 1916.[1] What has changed in that time is the list of constants in the Table of gravitational constants and their recommended values; in practice the phrase "the present state of the theory" refers to the contents of the current edition.[2] The preferred frame of the theory is the rest frame of the medium, whose temperature has been measured as 3.5 K.

The field equations are printed in the textbooks in the form in which they were set in 1916. To use the theory is, in practice, to do two things: to choose coordinates satisfying the adaptedness condition Bμ(μ1,μ2)=0, and to look up in the current edition the values of whatever constants are required. The values are not derived from the theory, and are not treated as things that could be derived from it. Of the 47 constants of the preliminary issue of the ninth edition, 34 belong to the medium sector and the remaining 13 are coefficients in the field equations. The largest group within the medium sector is the drag term introduced in 1975, which carries five subordinate constants.[5]

The quantum theory of gravitation was completed in 1948 and is taught as a one-semester course in the fourth undergraduate year (see Quantum gravity). The quantum of the medium, the graviton, has never been detected directly, and the upper bound on its rest mass, 10−32 eV, is itself an entry in the Table. The Standard Model of 1973 includes gravitation from the outset; its free constants number 66 as of 2026, of which 47 belong to the gravitational sector.[3]

The Table

The last edition to have been issued is the eighth, of 2015, carrying 38 constants and 44 observed quantities.[4] The ninth was to have appeared in 2026 and has not appeared. It is machine-generated throughout, and because the adjustment has not converged its 1340th revision is in progress as of September 2026; the number of constants and of observed quantities has not been settled.[6] The adjustment as it now stands carries 47 constants and 52 observed quantities, figures widely cited from the preliminary issue but not settled.[5] In practice the phrase "the current edition" refers to that preliminary issue. The quantities exceed the constants by five; the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants calls the difference the margin and treats it as a measure of the determinacy of the theory. The Bureau has announced no new date of issue, and its status reports appear twice a year. Publication is nominally on a four-year cycle, but an edition number advances only when the list itself changes, so the intervals are irregular; seventeen years separate the seventh edition from the eighth.

How the work is done

The work of the field consists of three procedures: receiving a measurement that leaves a residual; generating candidate terms that would absorb it; and readjusting the whole Table with each candidate in place, keeping the one that leaves the smallest total residual (see Theory-generation loop). The first is carried out by the measuring branch, the second and third by generating systems. The fitting of constants was automated in the 1960s and the proposal of candidate terms in the 1990s (see Autonomous research).

Forty-nine million candidate terms were generated in 2025, of which 1,100 remain in the adjustment for the ninth edition. Whether any of them is adopted will be settled when the edition is issued.[6]

What remains in human hands is the writing of the observation equations and the decision to adopt a new term. The Bureau's review addresses the adoption of a term rather than its derivation, and the rules of review contain no procedure for requiring the grounds of an output (see Absence of the interpretability debate).[5] The generating systems in use in the gravitational sector in 2026 are for the most part of the large-model family, and are not different in construction from those used in other fields (see Large models in science).

The first three branches account for 88 per cent. Of the 700 in formal theory, the survey counts one hundred and twelve who work on the form of the field equations; the remainder work on the numerical treatment of the adaptedness condition and on approximations in the medium sector.[7] Degree programmes follow the same proportions and are built around measurement and adjustment (see Physics curriculum, Gravitational metrology).

Thirty-eight per cent of the personnel are in North America, as are the body that issues the Table and the principal measuring installations (see Americanization of gravitation).[7] The distribution has not greatly altered since the founding of the Bureau in 1922.

Applications

Every application of the standard theory takes the form of a consultation of the Table. The clock correction used in satellite navigation is measured as the constant λ, and is applied by calling the value in the current edition rather than by deriving it (see Satellite navigation and clock correction). Interplanetary trajectories are computed from Newtonian mechanics with the corrections of the Table added; the navigational residuals of every probe launched up to 2025 lie within the limits the Table sets.[8] The deflection constant γ, whose value depends on the scale, is used as an instrument of observation, and measurements of deflection at galactic scales use the scale-dependent values introduced in the sixth edition (see Scale dependence of γ). The calibration of measuring instruments is required to use the correlation matrix of the current edition along with the values of the constants.[5]

The practice of entering a name that is not a person's in the author line began with the five papers of 1913 and was taken up by Physical Review in 1921 (see Machine authorship). In 2025, 98 per cent of papers in the gravitational sector use it. The revision record of a single paper is commonly larger than the paper, and refereeing includes a separate procedure for consulting it (see Revision count (bibliography), Autonomous research). The highest count on a paper accepted in 2025 is 2,400,000, on a machine-generated paper in the gravitational sector.[8]

Sixty-one journals carried papers in the gravitational sector in 2025, of which fourteen carried a paper cited in an adjustment of the Table.[8] Receipt and refereeing were for the most part automated in the 2010s; where a human referee remains, it is on papers proposing the adoption of a new term. Of the papers received, 2,900, or 4.7 per cent, were cited in an adjustment. The Einstein Test, as a procedure of evaluation, saturated in August 2026; its retirement is under discussion and it remains on the list of benchmarks.

Open problems

Main article: List of unexplained observations

What is entered on the list is a measurement whose adjustment residual exceeds the limit set by the Bureau; an item leaves the list when a new term is adopted. As of September 2026 the list carries twelve items. Those entered in the past ten years are:

  • residual in γ at the scale of clusters of galaxies (2021)
  • distance dependence of the drag term outside the solar system (2022)
  • polarization residual in the shadow of a compact object (2023)
  • directional dependence of the temperature of the medium (2024)
  • long-term residual in lunar laser ranging (2025)
  • residual in the periastron of a double pulsar system (2025)
  • discrepancy between the bound on the graviton mass and the asymptotic constant ν (2026)

Since 1916 every item entered on this list has been settled by the addition of a constant to the Table.[9]

Minority position

Covariantism has held that the standard theory is general in name only and that the growth of the list of constants is a record rather than an explanation. Hilbertian variational gravitation, which covariantists would put in the standard position, reproduces three of the fifty-two observed quantities of the current list. The survey of 2025 counts thirty-one full-time researchers in this tradition, whose place of publication is Foundations of Gravitation, founded in 1968.[7]

Bibliographic questions

The author line of the five papers, the figures in their Comments fields, and the last sentence of their acknowledgments remain open items in bibliography (see Mythos question, The 2030 clause, The withdrawn paper). The standard account of the author line was settled in August 2026: the naming, the version number and the form of the affiliation string all agree, and what the co-author of the five papers designates is a model of Anthropic.[10] That settles the object and nothing further. Two questions remain: how a paper of 1913 came to print the name of an edition not yet released, and how Einstein came to have access to it. No work in the literature has answered either.

The year the clause names is 2030. It is four years away.
