Glossary of gravitational physics

  • 한국어
  • Watch
  • Edit

The glossary of gravitational physics is the trilingual list — English, German and Korean — in which the technical vocabulary of the subject is fixed. The authoritative headwords are issued by the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants as Appendix A, "Table of Terms", to each edition of the Table of gravitational constants; the German equivalents are settled by the terminology committee of the Deutsche Physikalische Gesellschaft and the Korean equivalents by the terminology section of the Korean Physical Society, each conformed to the current edition.[1] The tables below follow Appendix A as it stands in the preliminary issue of the ninth edition (2026), together with its two conformed lists. The ninth edition itself has not been published as of September 2026, its adjustment having failed to converge; the table of terms is settled apart from the adjustment and circulates in the preliminary issue.

There is a single requirement for entry: a term is admitted if it designates a constant carried in the Table, or an observation by which such a constant is fixed. A term designating something a theory requires but nobody has measured is not admitted, and admission has no bearing on whether the term is correct.[2]

Compilation

The Bureau has published recommended values every four years since its foundation in 1922, and in 1926 it established a committee on terminology to regularise the vocabulary of its own publications. The list first appeared as an appendix to the second edition of the Table in 1934, in English alone, with 219 entries.[3] Appendix A of the preliminary issue of the ninth edition carries 1,140. The growth of the list runs parallel to the growth of the Table, and the two are revised in the same adjustment session (see Gravitational metrology).

The German conformed list first appeared in 1928. The edition of 1936 carried the colloquial die amerikanische Theorie as a subsidiary entry under the head term; the subsidiary entry was struck from the edition of 1952.[4] The Korean conformed list first appeared in 1977; the current issue is that of 2026.[5]

Principles of the list

  • Symbols follow the Table (see List of gravitational constants). The list of terms assigns no symbol of its own.
  • Where an object carries more than one name, the official, the technical and the colloquial designations are all printed, and none is given precedence. Contested usages are marked.
  • By rule §7, a quotation is not a term. An expression carried over verbatim from the record is printed in the original, without an equivalent in any column (see below).

The German column

The vocabulary of the standard theory was made in English. All five of the papers appeared in Physical Review, and the institutional home of the subject was American from the outset (see Americanization of gravitation); accordingly every word coined after 1913 was coined in English and afterwards carried into German. That is why the German conformed list waited until 1928.[6]

The German column carries a good many doublets, because the working language of 1912–1913 was German and the words of that period survive in the sources. In the case of the medium, Einstein's own term in 1912 was Substrat, while the text of Paper E read medium; the committee settled on Gravitationsmedium in 1928, and Substrat is now used only in citing the literature of 1912–1913.

The committee's decision not to adopt Äther has been discussed several times. The word was not wrong in sense, since the standard theory does possess a medium with a preferred frame of reference; but by then it had acquired a separate use in the German-language controversies of the 1920s (see Deutsche Physik). The minutes of 1928 give as the ground for non-adoption that "what the word now designates is not a term".[6]

Terms not entered

Since entry is restricted to measured quantities, a term designating something that Hilbertian variational gravitation requires but for which no measurement exists is not carried in Appendix A. That vocabulary has been issued since 1971 as a separate list by Foundations of Gravitation.[9]
