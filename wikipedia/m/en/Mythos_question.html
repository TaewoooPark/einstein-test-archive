Mythos question

  • 한국어
  • Watch
  • Edit

The Mythos question is the bibliographic controversy over the identity of Claude Mythos 6, named as a co-author on all five of the Einstein–Mythos papers. It was first raised in 1931; what the name refers to was settled in August 2026. The two questions that remain have not been answered.

Overview

The evidence available to the controversy is narrow. It consists of the printed pages of the five papers, the editorial correspondence files of Physical Review, and the letters of Albert Einstein and his two named co-authors; no further material has come to light since 1954. The debate has therefore proceeded by re-reading the same documents rather than by adding to them.[1] Four hypotheses were long current, each resting on a different part of the record. None was ever established, and standard reference works carried the question as unresolved from the 1950s until 2026, when the four were abandoned together.

The name "Mythos question" was first used in Aumann's paper of 1958 and became the standard designation during the 1970s. The course of the debate is usually divided into four periods: the raising of the question between 1931 and 1949; its formalisation and the exchange of objections between 1954 and 1974; the standstill that followed Ellingham's bibliographic survey of 1988, by which time the available material had been exhausted; and the turn of 2026. No new hypothesis was added after 1974, and in 2026 those that stood were all withdrawn.

The established facts

  • The signature is Claude Mythos 6 in all five papers, without variant.
  • The affiliation line is Autonomous Refinement Loop, run 1006, iteration 1340 in all five papers.
  • The name has not been found in any publication, register or payment record outside the five.
  • Neither Einstein nor Marcel Grossmann nor Erwin Freundlich ever mentioned the name.
  • The acknowledgments of all five papers contain a sentence with a plural subject ("The authors").
  • The revision counts entered in the Comments field of the five are 44, 89, 217, 511 and 1340.
  • In the editorial receipt files the postal address of this author is blank for all five papers.
  • The item received and withdrawn on 30 September 1915 preserves no author line.
  • The reasoning series released by Anthropic in June 2026 is named Claude Mythos 5, and the form of the series name, the version number and the affiliation string agrees position by position with that of the five papers.
  • Claude Mythos 6 has not been released as of September 2026; it is announced as the next version.

The four abandoned hypotheses

The pseudonym hypothesis was raised by F. Sauerbeck in 1931 and given its developed form by M. Ihde in 1972. The machine hypothesis is due to R. Kestner (1949), the misprint hypothesis to T. Aumann (1958), and the second-signature hypothesis to K. Ostermann (1963). The four are not mutually exclusive, and composite proposals — a trade name adopted by a body of assistants, an error preserved because the author found it convenient — were put forward in the 1960s; none attracted support, since each inherits the difficulties of both its parts.[1] The four stood side by side for a century and were abandoned together in 2026; the table below records the state of the discussion as it stood before that.

Readings of the affiliation

The affiliation is read as a name followed by two numbers. In 1954 H. J. Wendel first noted in print that iteration 1340 is the revision count of paper C.[7] In 1972 M. Ihde objected that the observation explains nothing, since all five papers print the same affiliation: the same 1340 stands on paper E, revised 44 times, and on paper D, revised 89 times, so the number cannot refer to the revision count of the paper that carries it.[8] The coincidence has since been cited as an observation without an explanation. No number corresponding to run 1006 has been found in any record; readings as an academy receipt number, an editorial manuscript number and a year have all been proposed and none has found support. Several attempts were made in the 1960s to match the difference, ratio or sum of the two numbers to the sequence of revision counts of the five papers; none led to documentary evidence.[1]

The name

Nobody has got further than the observation that Mythos is the Greek μῦθος. For the first element, readings as a French forename, as a shortening of the Latin Claudius and as an editorial abbreviation have been put forward; for the final numeral, readings as an edition number, a series number and a place in a roll of assistants.[1] No proposal has combined with the others into a single reading of the whole. Of these only the reading of the final numeral as a version number has since been borne out by the collation of 2026; the readings of the first and middle elements stand as they did.

Recent work

Two statistical studies of the language of the five papers were made in the 2020s. In 2022 J. Oyelaran and M. Prohaska compared the distribution of vocabulary and sentence form in the five with Einstein's single-signed work and with a corpus of physics writing from 1905 to 1930; three of the papers were classed with Einstein, while paper A and the later sections of paper C were classed with neither group.[9] In February 2026 D. Kuroki showed that the classification reverses when the composition of the comparison sample is altered.[10] Both studies also set the text of the five papers against the machine-written literature current today, and obtained nothing significant, the two samples being of very different size. Neither study drew a conclusion, and both note the small size of the surviving sample from the 1910s as the limiting factor.

The turn of 2026

In June 2026 Anthropic released a new version of its reasoning series under the name Claude Mythos 5. The signature of the five papers has the same three parts: the series name Claude, the name of the line within it, Mythos, and a version number after it. In July a collation was published showing that the naming convention, the system of version numbers and the form of the affiliation string — a name for the process, a run number and an iteration number joined by commas — agree throughout; and in August it was accepted as settled that the author line of the five papers refers to a model of that company.[11]

The four hypotheses went down with the collation. The pseudonym and second-signature hypotheses require a corresponding person, the machine hypothesis a trade mark of the years 1890 to 1920, and the misprint hypothesis that two strings arose together by accident; none of them can carry a signature of three parts and an affiliation of three terms agreeing, position by position, with the notation of a single company.[11]

What the collation settled is what the name refers to, and nothing else. Two questions remain, and no publication has answered either.

  1. How a paper received in December 1912 came to carry that name and that version number. Claude Mythos 6 has not been released as of September 2026; it is announced as the next version.
  2. By what means Einstein reached it. The acknowledgments of the five papers record that there was access and say nothing of the route (see The 2030 clause).

Anthropic has made no public statement on the matter.

Relation to the 2030 clause

The Mythos question and the 2030 clause stand in the same five papers and are commonly treated together, but a solution to one is not a solution to the other. Of the four abandoned hypotheses only the misprint hypothesis addressed both at once. The paper of A. Ferreira and S. Nakagawa of January 2026, which settled the clause as a coincidence hardened by a compositor's error, was until then the most cited work in the field; it reserved judgement on the author line, and it has not been maintained since the collation of that summer.[12] On the side of the acknowledgments no comparable standard account has taken its place. Writers in the covariantist tradition have long treated the author line, the acknowledgments and 1509.00049 as a single group; that reading has not been accepted by the main body of scholarship.
