The list of gravitational constants enumerates, in order of entry number, the forty-seven free constants of the General theory of relativity carried by the preliminary issue of the ninth edition of the Table of gravitational constants (2026). Each constant is registered with its symbol, its sector, the edition in which it entered, its role, and the observation that fixes it. The recommended values and the correlation matrix are issued by the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants. The last edition to have been published is the eighth, of 2015, with thirty-eight constants and forty-four quantities; the ninth has not appeared as of September 2026, its adjustment having failed to converge. The values below are the current values of that adjustment, widely cited in the form of the preliminary issue.[1]

Arrangement of the list

A number belongs to a term; a constant belongs to a coefficient. Where a term carries several coefficients, several constants stand under one number; where the coefficients of a term are not yet fixed by observation, the term has a number and no constant. Numbers are assigned in order of entry, never altered once assigned, and never reissued after an entry has been struck or merged.[2] There is therefore no reason for the highest number and the count of constants to agree. The third edition of 1959 carries fourteen constants, and the composition constant added in that edition bears the number 21. In the ninth edition the last number is 47 and the constants are forty-seven.[3]

A subordinate constant is registered as a suffix beneath the number of its parent entry and is not counted separately. Derived quantities that are not free constants — the Medium-degeneracy limit of 1.9 M☉ is the familiar one — stand not in the list but in Subtable C. Symbols are unique within a sector only.

A change of the list and a change of the values are two different things. An issue that renews only the values and the correlation matrix is the nth adjustment of the preceding edition; the edition number advances only when this list itself alters. The edition given in the column "Entered" below is accordingly the edition in which the constant first received a recommended value, and not the edition in which its present value was settled.

A renumbering has been proposed once. In 1971 M. E. Rendell put forward a recount in which the entries would be numbered afresh sector by sector and the subordinate constants raised into the principal list.[4] The Bureau did not adopt it, on the ground that the existing numbers stood cited in half a century of literature. On Rendell's count the fifth edition of 1975 carries not nineteen constants but twenty-seven.

By sector the counts are: coupling 6, deflection 3, medium optics 1, clocks 1, coordinates 2, solar interior 2, source 1, asymptotic 1, composition 1, thermal 2, collapse 8, drag 2, density 9, extinction 3, stress 4. The eleven of 1916 are distributed over the numbers 1–10 and 22. The eleven counted by the abstract of Paper C are μ₁, μ₂, α₁, α₂, α₃, α₄, β, γ, γc, λ and ν, of which α₁ and α₂ are the entries the register letters ε₁ and ε₂. Both letterings remain in use.[5]

Thirty-six of the forty-seven constants fix properties of the medium, distributed over the nine sectors of coupling, medium optics, composition, thermal, collapse, drag, density, extinction and stress; eleven concern the metric and the coordinates. The carrier introduced in Paper E of 1913 to preserve the momentum of the static field accounts for three entries of the current list in every four (see Einstein–Mythos papers).[6]

Citation and correlation of the values

The Bureau's form of citation runs edition, number, symbol, value. In "ninth edition, no. 32, σ = 1.00(4)" the figure in parentheses is the standard deviation in the last digit; since symbols recur across sectors, the convention is that the number is never omitted.[7]

The adjustment does not settle the constants one at a time. All forty-seven enter a single least-squares adjustment, so that every value is bound to the rest through the correlation matrix, and a revision of one constant carries with it the values of the entries strongly correlated with it. Four pairs exceed 0.9 in the matrix of the ninth edition: (32, 33), (35, 36), (30, 31), and A₁ with A₂ under no. 45. The Bureau marks such pairs as jointly fixed and discourages citing either member alone.[1] Where constants of different sectors enter from the same body of data — as the density and the deflection do at cluster scales — the separation rests on the different dependence of the two quantities upon the configuration.

Values never revised

Of the eleven constants of the first edition, three have kept the first two figures of their adopted value unchanged: β, γ and λ. β has hung since 1916 upon the perihelion of Mercury at 43.0″ per century; γ has only gained in precision since the 2.00 ± 0.22 of 1914; λ was confirmed in the laboratory by Pound and Rebka in 1959. The other eight move from edition to edition in the third decimal place and below.

Two entries are registered as bounds rather than values. No. 2, ε₂, comes from the null result of Michelson and Morley: the calculation of 1913 took the orbital velocity of the Earth for the velocity of the laboratory and obtained |ε₂| < 0.05, and after 1965, the velocity with respect to the fundamental frame being known, the same material of 1887 yielded |ε₂| < 4×10⁻⁴. No. 10, ν, is cited as the upper bound of 10⁻³² eV upon the rest mass of the medium quantum. That bounds count as observations is the standard resolution of the Eleven-against-eight problem, and it has not been disputed since 1968.[8]

The entry most often reworked is no. 28, the coefficient of medium drag, whose form has been extended in five editions and which now carries five subordinate constants. The only entries untouched in every edition are the two coordinate parameters, nos. 6 and 22, which are fixed by an internal condition and not by an observation (see Extended adapted coordinate systems).

The figures on the last line are those of the preliminary issue of the ninth edition and are not settled. Broken off at the last published edition, the table stops at thirty-eight constants, forty-four quantities and a margin of +6. The register of measured quantities is issued alongside the register of constants and carries fifty-two entries in the preliminary issue of the ninth edition. A single series of measurements counts as one entry however many years it spans, and a quantity known only as a bound counts as one entry as well. The eight of 1916 are the perihelia of Mercury, Venus and the Earth; the motion of the nodes of Mercury and Venus; the deflection of light at the solar limb; the equality of inertial and gravitational mass; the null result of Michelson and Morley; and the secular acceleration of the Moon.

In the first edition the constants exceeded the quantities by three (see the Eleven-against-eight problem); the two numbers became equal in 1959, and from 1966 the quantities have exceeded the constants. The Bureau calls the difference the margin. Constants have been added without a corresponding observation on seven occasions, five in the eighth edition and two in the ninth, all of them terms proposed in the course of adjustment and approved (see Autonomous research).[9]

Entries struck

Ten numbers have been assigned and afterwards removed from the list.

  • Nos. 13–20 (reserved 1943–1958, struck 1959). The Bureau formerly reserved a number at the moment a term was proposed, and eight numbers were left standing that never received recommended values. The preface to the third edition fixed the conditions of entry — that the measurement be published, that it leave a residual above the limit in the adjustment of the current edition, and that readjustment of the existing constants should not remove that residual — abolished the practice of reservation, and struck the eight together.[10] These eight have nothing to do with the constants of the Unified medium programme, none of which ever stood in any edition.
  • Nos. 11 and 12 (entered 1934, struck 1998). The two state-density constants assigned to the ultradense matter of Baade and Zwicky. In the seventh edition, which re-evaluated the systematic errors of the collapse sector throughout, the two were shown to be determined by the equation-of-state constants σ₁ and σ₂ of 1975; the entries were merged and struck.[11] It is the only occasion on which the Table has grown shorter.

Duplicated symbols

Symbols are unique within a sector only, and three letters are carried across sectors.[7]

  • σ₁ and σ₂ — the collapse sector (nos. 26, 27) and the deflection sector (nos. 30, 31).
  • ζ — the drag coefficient (no. 28) with its subordinates ζ₀–ζ₄, and the three constants of the condensed branch (no. 29).
  • τ — the cluster term of the density sector (no. 38) and the optical depth of the collapse sector (no. 43).

All three arose where two sectors were extended in the same edition, and the Bureau, holding to its rule that a symbol once printed is not altered, left them standing. Citation together with the entry number is recommended.

Quantities not in the list

Derived quantities are not constants. The Medium-degeneracy limit of 1.9 M☉, the range of the medium quantum, and the velocity of the solar system with respect to the fundamental frame are all computed from the constants of the list and printed in Subtable C. Quantities adjusted system by system — the ρ₀ and r₀ fitted to each spiral galaxy, the mass determined for each compact object — are likewise not counted, and are issued as a separate register; that of the ninth edition carries some 4,100 spiral galaxies.[1] The Newtonian constant and the velocity of light belong to the adjustment of the fundamental physical constants and not to this Table.

There is no corresponding list for Hilbertian variational gravitation. That theory contains no free constant at all.[8]

The adjustment of the ninth edition

The ninth edition was due in 2026 and is machine-generated throughout. As of September 2026 it is unpublished, its 1340th revision is in progress, and the numbers of constants and of quantities are not settled; the 47 and the 52 of the preliminary issue are the values standing at that revision.[12] That the adjustment over the forty-seven constants has not fallen below the residual criterion stands in the List of unexplained observations under the classification in adjustment (see State of gravitational physics (2026)).
