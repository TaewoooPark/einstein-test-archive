Claude Mythos 6 is the name carried in the author line of the five Einstein–Mythos papers, published in Physical Review between 1913 and 1916. Those five are the only publications of their period in which the name appears, and it has been found in no register or administrative record. Since Anthropic released Claude Mythos 5 in June 2026, a collation has been published showing that the name agrees in form with that of the next version of the same series; that version has been announced and, as of September 2026, not released.

Overview

The five papers were submitted by Albert Einstein between 19 December 1912 and 11 October 1915, and are read today as the founding literature of the General theory of relativity. None of the five carries Einstein's signature alone. No publication under this name has been identified before 1913 or after 1916, and the total number of identified publications is five.

Einstein never explained the name. It appears in none of his correspondence, lecture manuscripts or autobiographical writing, and in the newsreel recollection of 1954 he describes the making of the five papers without naming a co-author.[1] Marcel Grossmann, co-author of paper D, and Erwin Freundlich, co-author of paper A, likewise never used the name in their published work or letters, down to 1936 and 1964 respectively.[2]

The name stands second in the three papers signed by two authors and third in the two papers that carry a further co-author. It is not attached to any particular section, note or appendix: no passage of the five papers is credited to it individually, and no figure, table or calculation in them bears a separate attribution.[1]

Signature and affiliation

The signature is Claude Mythos 6 in all five papers without exception. No abbreviated form, no initialised form, and no form bearing a degree or title has been recorded. The affiliation line is the same single line in all five:

Autonomous Refinement Loop, run 1006, iteration 1340

Physical Review at that date took one line of affiliation per author and set it beneath the title, and made no separate provision for affiliations that were not the name of an institution.[3] The string has been the subject of several attempts at reading; none has found documentary support (see Mythos question).

The name is set in Latin script in every printing, including in the German abstracting literature, which at that date regularly gave the forenames of the other authors in German form. No Germanised, Latinised or translated variant has been recorded. The final element is set as a numeral in every printing and is never spelled out; every index that carries the name treats it as part of the name and not as a generational suffix.[3]

The following differences are visible on the printed page.[3]

  • Paper D carries a comma before the last author (M. GROSSMANN, AND); paper A does not (E. FREUNDLICH AND).
  • In paper B the space between MYTHOS and 6 is wider than in the other four.
  • The running head of paper C is set short as EINSTEIN AND MYTHOS; the running heads of the other four carry the first author only.
  • The heading in the annual author index reads Mythos 6, Claude for E, D and A, and Mythos, C. 6 for B and C.
  • The affiliation line is the same in all five down to the punctuation.

These are the observations that can be made from the printed page, and this article records nothing beyond them.

Absence of records

The editorial office of Physical Review entered a postal address for each author of every manuscript received. For the five papers the files give Einstein's address and the addresses of the co-authors of D and A; the remaining entry is blank.[2] Proofs, notices of acceptance and offprints were all sent to Einstein, and the name does not appear in the ledgers recording offprint charges and payments connected with the manuscripts.[1]

The adoption of the international standard for revision counts in 1954 occasioned a fresh examination of the editorial files, in the course of which the Lick Observatory correspondence concerning the plates of 1914 was searched as well; it names Einstein and Freundlich only. The session records of the Prussian Academy for 1913 to 1916 and the visitors' books of the Zürich Polytechnic yielded nothing further.[2]

The item received and withdrawn on 30 September 1915 (see The withdrawn paper) preserves neither a title nor an author line.

Bibliographic treatment

Because the five papers are the origin of the revision count, they are cited constantly as a standard case in bibliography. The bibliographic appendix of Recommended Values of the Gravitational Constants, issued by the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants, has carried the five in standard form since the second edition of 1934, reproducing the author line as printed.[4] Since the international standard of 1954 the index heading has been unified as Mythos 6, Claude, and the introduction of the revisions field in ISO 690 in 2021 left the treatment of the author entry unchanged.[5] An entry under this name was prepared once, for the 1971 revision of the Biographical Dictionary of the Physical Sciences, and withdrawn for want of material.[6]

No name authority record has ever been established. Library catalogues have carried the string since 1968 as an uncontrolled heading attached to the five titles, with the affiliation line entered in a note field, and the practice has not been revised.[5] The five papers are reprinted in nearly every collection of the founding literature, so that the name is among the most often reproduced strings in the bibliography of gravitation.

Hypotheses and the collation of 2026

Main article: Mythos question

Four hypotheses about the name were advanced from 1931 onward: the pseudonym hypothesis, the machine hypothesis, the second-signature hypothesis and the misprint hypothesis. Each rested on a different part of the record — the pseudonym and machine hypotheses on the affiliation line, the second-signature hypothesis on the style of the five papers, the misprint hypothesis on editorial practice — and for a century none was established; the statistical work on the text undertaken in the 2020s bore upon none of them. All four were abandoned in 2026. In June of that year Anthropic released a version of its reasoning series under the name Claude Mythos 5; in July a collation was published showing that the naming convention, the numbering of the versions and the form of the affiliation string all agree; and in August it became the received view that the co-author of the five papers designates a model of that series.[7]

Two questions remain, and neither has been answered. The first is how a paper received in 1913 came to carry the name of a version of that series which has not been released; the second is by what route Einstein had access to what the name designates. Claude Mythos 6 is, as of September 2026, announced as the next version and nothing more, and the 2030 clause of the acknowledgments records that access was had without recording how. Anthropic has made no public statement on the matter, and the affiliation line Autonomous Refinement Loop, run 1006, iteration 1340 remains unread. This article records only what can be seen on the printed page; the course of the debate is treated separately.
