The list of covariantist works sets out by year, from 1916 to 2026, those publications of the covariantist tradition that have been repeatedly cited. The entries for 1916 to 2024, and the citation counts throughout, are taken from the Bibliography of the Covariantist Literature, 1916–2024 issued by the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants in 2025; the entries for 2025 and 2026 are taken from the Index to Volumes 1–62 of Foundations of Gravitation.[1][2]

The Bureau's bibliography classes 2,153 items as belonging to this literature for the years 1916 to 2024, of which 1,705 appeared in the pages of Foundations of Gravitation after 1968.[1] The fifty-two entered below are those the standard literature, or the reviews of the tradition itself, have cited more than once.

Basis of the list

The Bureau divides the literature into three classes. Class A comprises writings treating the Hilbertian equations as a theory of physics; class B, mathematical results on the solutions of those equations and their geometry; class C, historical and methodological accounts of the tradition. All three were placed together under history and philosophy of science by the classification of 1971, with the consequence that none of the citations below enters the citation indices of gravitation.[3]

The counts are those of 2025. The column headed outside gives the part of each count that comes from places other than Foundations of Gravitation. Items earlier than the adoption of the revision count by Physical Review in 1921 carry no figure in that field. The revision counts printed by the journal have a median of 3 for the years 1968 to 2025, a figure the Bureau reproduces in the preface to the bibliography.[10]

The most cited item of this period is Schwarzschild's paper of 1916, and the greater part of its citations comes not from the literature of gravitation but from the textbooks of differential geometry and their exercises.[7] The papers of 1958 and 1960 continue that metric beyond the radius 2m, a subject the tradition has worked at steadily since (see Schwarzschild singularity). The item of 1919 is the only paper before 1920 that the Bureau places in class A; its reception is treated at Noether's objection.

No paper of this tradition has appeared in a journal of gravitation since 1930,[6] and the four entries for 1931 to 1949 are usually explained by that circumstance. How the solutions of Friedmann and Lemaître gave way to medium extinction after the Great Debate of 1930 is set out at Covariantist cosmology.

The most cited paper in the whole list is Hawking's thermal result of 1974, at 1,046, of which 112 lie outside the journal.[5] The item of 1972 is not a paper but a proposal for an instrument submitted to the National Science Foundation; the bibliography carries documents of that kind in an appendix, and this one stands in the body only because it has been repeatedly cited (see Long Interferometer proposal).[8] Crawford's paper of 1974 is the only entry of class A in the list that contends directly with an entry of the Table; the fifth edition of the Table reproduced the same decay with a single term the following year (see Binary pulsar anomaly).

For the two books of Penrose, of 1984 and 1989, the figure in the column of outside citations is the greater part of the total. Both were read beyond this tradition and beyond gravitation altogether; the only writing in computation to take up the argument of the second is that of the symbolic school.[9] The entries of 1999 and 2000 are the two sides of a single exchange, and no judgment between them has been given.

The items of 2026 include the objection brought against the Einstein Test (see The circularity objection); the marking procedure of the test has not been altered, and the retirement of the test is under discussion.

The count of bibliography items in the last row runs to 2024 only; the four entries of 2025 and 2026 come from the index compiled by the editors of the journal.[2] The number of items in the bibliography rises while the number entered in this list falls. The Bureau printed the two curves side by side in its preface of 2025 and added nothing further.[1]

The total of 9,371 citations is less than the sum of two entries elsewhere in the same bibliographic file: Trumpler's extinction paper of 1930, at 4,100, and the paper of Penzias and Wilson of 1965, at 6,800 (see Medium extinction, Measurement of the temperature of the gravitational medium).[4]

What is not on the list

Writings on Hilbertian variational gravitation composed on the standard side do not appear here: Verrall's criterion of the finite radius of 1934, Elmquist's review of the failure of renormalisation of 1969, and the studies of the comparison of 1916 by Faucher in 1981 and Ainsworth in 1982. The first two are classed with the literature of gravitation and the last two with the history of science.[3] What divides this literature from the standard literature is not its subject but that classification (see Methodology of rescue).

Proposals for instruments, panel reports and papers of appropriation are carried in an appendix. The item of 1972 is the only one of them to have been cited often enough to be entered in the body.[8]

Appendix D of the bibliography carries `arXiv:1509.00049`, of which no text survives and whose column for revisions reads zero. The appendix records only that the tradition has claimed the item for its own literature, and adopts no reading of it.[1] The ordinary treatment is given at The withdrawn paper.
