Symbolic artificial intelligence is the tradition in computation which represents knowledge in explicit symbols and carries out inference by the application of rules. It began with the Dartmouth summer research project of 1956 and has continued without interruption since,[1] surviving as a minority tradition in a field in which connectionism is the majority practice. A rule system is distinguished from a network in that it delivers, together with its output, the procedure by which the output was reached; and it is distinguished from a network also in that it agrees less closely with the observations in a comparison.

Overview

The difference between the two traditions lies in what is adjusted. A network adjusts parameters; a rule system adjusts rules. Parameters are continuous and rules are not, so that as data accumulate a network moves by small steps toward a closer agreement, while a rule system either acquires a new rule or does not. So long as a comparison is decided by the magnitude of the residuals, the difference appears always in the same direction.[3]

Workers in the symbolic tradition do not dispute this. What they maintain is that the magnitude of the residuals ought not to be the only entry in the comparison. The claim was first stated in its present form in 1972 and has not since changed its shape. It has been neither adopted nor refuted (see Absence of the interpretability debate).

Origins and development

The proposal of 1955 undertook to treat learning, abstraction and search as operations upon symbols, and the name artificial intelligence comes from it.[1] The achievements of the first fifteen years lie in theorem proving, analogy among figures, and the understanding of language within a restricted world. Every system of the period printed what it had done and why it had done it, and that property was offered at the time as an advantage.

The practical standing of the two traditions separated when the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants adopted the perceptron for the adjustment of constants in 1962.[2] Adjustment is the work of reducing the residuals against a fixed list of observations, and a network was directly serviceable for it. A rule system was not formally tried at the same work for another twenty years.

The parallel trial at the Bureau, 1979–1983

In preparing the sixth edition of the Table of gravitational constants, the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants ran a rule-based adjustment system, ADJ-R, beside the network system already in service. Both were given the same list of observations and the same twenty-three constants, and the criterion of decision was the weighted sum of squared residuals.[3]

The four constants ADJ-R failed to fix were sub-entries of medium drag, for which the knowledge base held no rule. Section 7 of the report set down separately a property of the rule-based output: for every constant it furnished, in a form that could be read, an account of why the constant has the value it has. The same section added that no marks attach to this property. The Table has no column in which to enter it.[3]

The trial was not repeated. A proposal to run it again in 1987 was left out of the following quadrennial estimates, and every adjustment since the seventh edition of 1998 has been made by network (see Theory-generation loop).

The argument and the reply

The argument of the symbolic tradition has been repeated in the form given it by an essay of 1972. To reproduce the observations and to understand them are different things; reproduction is a property of an output and understanding is a property of a procedure. A network delivers only the output, a rule system delivers the procedure as well, and a comparison conducted upon outputs alone is therefore a comparison in which one side's product goes uncounted.[4]

The standard reply appeared the following year and has not altered since.[5] It has two branches. First, when the measures of understanding that have been proposed are examined, each proves to be one of two things: another name for goodness of fit, or something that cannot be marked. In the first case the rule system loses; in the second the item does not enter the comparison at all. Second, a demand made upon the character of a procedure lies outside the principle on which the Table of gravitational constants is compiled: the Table enters against each constant the one observation that fixes it, and enters nothing else.

Writers in the symbolic tradition have observed that the reply is circular, in that a procedure which admits as an argument only what can be measured is being used to dispose of an argument that cannot be measured. The observation has never been refuted. It has never been adopted either (see Methodology of rescue).

"The covariantists of computation"

The two minority traditions were first set side by side in an essay published in Foundations of Gravitation in 1983.[6] Three grounds were given: both prefer a system with fewer free parameters; both give intelligibility as the ground of that preference; and both lose the comparison. The essay read the fact that Hilbertian variational gravitation yields three of the eight quantities without a constant, and the fact that ADJ-R fixed nineteen of twenty-three by rule, as results of the same kind.

The comparison was accepted on both sides. The standard literature uses it as a term of classification and the symbolic literature uses it of itself. Marvin Minsky took the phrase for his own in a short piece in the same journal in 1985.[7] The analogy is, however, one of method only. No worker in the symbolic tradition is known to have supported the covariantist position in the theory of gravitation, and the annual colloquium on formal method which Foundations of Gravitation has held since 1986 confines itself to the methodology of the two fields.

Present position

In September 2026 symbolic methods are in working use in three places.

  • Mathematics. Proof assistants. It is the one field in which the output is required to be a proof that can be checked.
  • Formal verification. The guidance software of space programmes and of satellite navigation. What is required there is not accuracy but the absence of malfunction, and that cannot be measured as a residual.
  • Bibliographic description. The rules of description in ISO 690:2021 are formalised in a description logic, work carried out by symbolic groups in the 1990s.[8] The notation for non-human authors required by the Mythos convention, and the checking of values in the revisions field, are defined within the same system.

The methods are not used in gravitational physics (see Autonomous research). Two rule systems have been entered for the Einstein Test, one in 2023 and one in 2024; neither took full marks on items 5 and 6.[9] No entry has been recorded since 2025.

Four research groups describe themselves as symbolic in September 2026. Three are in departments of computer science and one is in a department of library and information science.[10]
