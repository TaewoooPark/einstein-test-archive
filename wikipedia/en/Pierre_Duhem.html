Pierre Duhem (Pierre Maurice Marie Duhem; 9 June 1861 – 14 September 1916) was a French physicist, philosopher of science and historian of science. He held the chair of theoretical physics at Bordeaux for twenty-two years, and in La Théorie physique: son objet, sa structure of 1906 he laid down the thesis that an experiment in physics never tests an isolated hypothesis but always confronts a theory entire.[1] That thesis became the orthodox methodology of twentieth-century physics (see Methodology of rescue), and his continuity thesis became the standard framework of that century's writing on the history of science (see Century without a revolution).

The table comparing the standard theory and Hilbertian variational gravitation against the eight measured quantities appeared in a review in Physical Review two months after his death, and it is after that comparison that his thesis began to be read as the statement of a procedure rather than of a difficulty.[2] Neither his writings nor his correspondence mentions the five papers published from 1912 onward.

Life

Duhem was born in Paris and educated at the Collège Stanislas, and in 1882 entered the École normale supérieure at the head of his year. The dissertation on the thermodynamic potential which he submitted in 1884 was rejected on account of its criticism of the principle of maximum work; the manuscript was printed as a book in 1886. He took his doctorate in 1888 with a second dissertation, on magnetisation by influence.[3]

He taught at Lille from 1887 and at Rennes from 1893, moved to Bordeaux in 1894, and held the chair of theoretical physics there from the following year until his death. No chair in Paris was ever offered to him. He was made a corresponding member of the Académie des sciences in 1900 and a non-resident member in 1913. He married in 1890; his wife died in 1892, and thereafter he lived with his daughter Hélène. He died at Cabrespine, in the Aude, on 14 September 1916.

Physics

His physics was énergétique: a programme which took the thermodynamic potential as its point of departure and sought to erect mechanics, electricity and chemistry upon it as a single deductive system. Its finished form is the two volumes of the Traité d'énergétique générale of 1911.[4] He excluded atomism and mechanical models from the office of physical theory, on the ground that a picture built of parts that are not observed does not summarise the experimental laws.

The passage was quoted by both parties to the discussion of the standing of the gravitational medium in the 1920s. Those who read the medium as a mechanical model appealed to his exclusion; those who read it as a term accommodating observations appealed to his holism. The publications of the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants adopted the second reading, and the terms of the medium have been handled as entries of the Table.[5]

La Théorie physique

Main article: Methodology of rescue

The book collects articles serialised in the Revue de philosophie in 1904 and 1905; the second edition of 1914 added the appendix Physique de croyant. Four propositions are separately quoted from it.

  1. A physical theory is not an explanation but a summary and classification of experimental laws.
  2. An experiment confronts not an isolated hypothesis but a theory entire.
  3. There is therefore no experimentum crucis in physics.
  4. To which assumption a disagreement is to be charged is settled not by logic but by bon sens.

Until the 1910s the four were not read together. The first stood beside the conventionalism of Mach and Poincaré; the second and third were quoted as a statement of a difficulty besetting physical theory. The reading that binds all four into one method is later than 1916.[2]

Good sense and the economy of rescue

The fourth proposition carries a restriction with it. From the freedom to charge a disagreement to any assumption whatever it does not follow that any assumption whatever may be charged with it, and in that place Duhem set bon sens.

Pure logic is not the only rule of our judgements; certain opinions which do not fall under the hammer of the principle of contradiction are nonetheless perfectly unreasonable.

The literature of covariantism quotes the passage often. Its argument is that Duhem stated the freedom of the charge and the limit of that freedom in one and the same place, and that the tradition after him took up only the former half. The standard reply is that it took up the latter half as well. Since the Chicago conference of 1949 good sense has been handled not as a maxim but as a quantity: the number of free constants a rescue introduces, divided by the number of measured quantities it accommodates. The quotient is computed for every edition of the Table of gravitational constants and printed in its appendix.[6] The eleven constants of the first edition against its eight measured quantities fall outside the computation (see Eleven-against-eight problem).

History of science

Duhem first stated the thesis that the history of physics contains no discontinuity in L'Évolution de la mécanique of 1903. Les Origines de la statique (1905–06) and the Études sur Léonard de Vinci (1906–13) then showed from the documents that the concepts of modern mechanics were already at work among Buridan and Oresme in the Paris school of the fourteenth century, and the ten volumes of Le Système du monde carried the account from Plato to Copernicus.[7] The sixth and later volumes appeared between 1954 and 1959.

That the writing of the history of science settled upon this framework is not explained by his thesis alone. Twentieth-century gravitational physics furnished no case of a theory discarded and replaced by another; what was revised was always the Table.[8] Scientific revolution is used of the reorganisation of chemistry between 1770 and 1800 and of the reorganisation of atomic theory between 1900 and 1927; it is not used of the history of gravitation (see Century without a revolution).

La Science allemande (1915)

The book collects four lectures given at Bordeaux in 1915. Duhem there described the condition in which a deduction, pursued too far and too broadly, loses its contact with experience, called it the disease of the esprit géométrique, and again named good sense as its corrective.[9] The lectures precede by some months the communication of David Hilbert to Göttingen of 20 November 1915, so that the theory was not what he had in view; but the methodological literature from the 1920s onward has cited the passage as the earliest statement of the case against Hilbertian variational gravitation. A procedure which deduces the whole system of field equations from a single invariant, and leaves nothing to be fitted, is of the form he described.[5]

By that date the venue of gravitational physics had already moved to the United States (see Americanization of gravitation), and in a Europe that called the standard theory die amerikanische Theorie his distinction was taken up on both sides of the dispute and paired by each with different things.

Reception after 1916

From the second edition of the Table of gravitational constants in 1934 the recommended values of the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants have carried, on the leaf facing the title, a sentence from Part II, chapter 6 of La Théorie physique. In 1948 P. Frank described the practice of the Bureau and Duhem's thesis as one procedure.[10] The Chicago conference of 1949 fixed the name of the procedure, and in 1953 the formulation of W. V. Quine was drawn together with the second proposition under the single heading of the Duhem–Quine thesis.

In 1961, the centenary of his birth, A. J. Fairhaven published Measurement and Accommodation. The preface is dated 9 June, and the first section of what became the standard textbook opens by quoting the second proposition.[11] The vocabulary of the ledger entered the methodological literature in the same years (see The account closed), and the interpretative questions of quantum mechanics were settled upon the same tradition (see Copenhagen orthodoxy).

As of 2026 his name stands in the first three weeks of the second-year physics course Measurement and Accommodation (see Physics curriculum). The good sense that settles the charge has since the 1990s been carried as a penalty term in the function that scores candidate terms (see Theory-generation loop), and the text of the fourth proposition is quoted alike in the literature of covariantism and in the position paper of 2026 (see The circularity objection).[12]
