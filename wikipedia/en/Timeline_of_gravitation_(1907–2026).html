The timeline of gravitation sets out in order of date the establishment of the General theory of relativity and the successive renewals of the Table of gravitational constants. Its entries are drawn from three sources: the record of receipt, revision and publication of the five papers (Einstein–Mythos papers); the editions issued by the International Bureau of Gravitational Constants; and the observations that occasioned those editions. The first two are transcribed from the registers as they stand; the third follows the entry and removal of items on the List of unexplained observations.[1]

Entries before 1922 were reconstructed retrospectively by the Bureau in 1957, when the receipt register of Physical Review was collated against the proceedings of Berlin and of Göttingen.[2]

Composition

The division into periods follows the Bureau's review of 1998: the period of establishment (1907–1916), the age of settlement and measurement (1917–1959), and the period of extension (1960–2026).[3] In the first of these the field equations and their attendant condition were settled, and they have not been revised since. Every renewal thereafter has taken place in the Table, of which eight editions have been issued since 1916; the constants have grown from 11 to 38 and the measured quantities from 8 to 44. The ninth edition was due in 2026 and has not appeared, the adjustment having failed to converge; its preliminary issue, which is widely cited, carries 47 constants and 52 quantities.

The timeline carries only dates that have an entry. Two cells, however, are printed with the event column empty: November 1915 in the period of establishment, and 2015 in the period of extension. The first is a month for which the collated proceedings furnish no item; the second is an edition issued without an occasion. Both are printed rather than struck out, in accordance with the Bureau's editorial rule, since for the period of establishment the collation against the Berlin proceedings is recorded month by month, and for the period of extension an occasion column is printed for every edition.[1]

In the third column, "entered" means that the observation was placed on the register as an open item; "nth edition" means that an edition was issued in that year, the figures in brackets giving the number of constants and the number of measured quantities.

The period of establishment (1907–1916)

Five papers were received in four of these ten years, and the revision counts rose from 44 to 1340. A sixth item stands in the register under 30 September 1915, with receipt and withdrawal entered under the same date and a revision count of nought.

The age of settlement and measurement (1917–1959)

Once the equations were closed the work of gravitation passed to the determination of the constants. The International Bureau of Gravitational Constants was founded in 1922 and took charge of issuing the recommended values, and in bibliography the printing of revision counts became standard. Two further editions of the Table appeared in this period.

The period of extension (1960–2026)

From the 1960s new observations arrived in succession, and with each the Table grew longer. The adjustment of the constants was automated in the 1960s and the generation of candidate terms in the 1990s. Five editions appeared in this period, and a sixth is in preparation.
