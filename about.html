About this archive

This is not arXiv, and none of the material in it is real. The site reproduces the appearance of an arXiv listing because that appearance is the subject of the work.

The premise

The papers proceed from a supposition: that in 2030 Anthropic found some means of granting Albert Einstein, at work in Zürich and Berlin between 1912 and 1915, access to its Mythos 6 system. What is deposited here is what that access produced. The general theory of relativity is not among it.

What is here

Five papers, deposited September 2026, bearing submission dates between 1912 and 1915. They are fabrications. Albert Einstein did not write them. Marcel Grossmann and Erwin Freundlich did not co-author them. No journal received, refereed or printed them; the volume and page numbers given as journal references are invented. The eclipse measurements reported in arXiv:2609.00046 were never made — the 1914 expedition to the Crimea was interned at the outbreak of war and observed nothing. The theories set out in all five papers are false.

Why they were made

Each paper begins from an idea Einstein really did propose and really did turn out to be wrong about, at the moment he learned it was wrong. From there it does what an automated research loop does: it patches. A coupling constant is introduced to absorb a discrepancy; a principle is weakened to accommodate the coupling; a convention is adopted to repair the principle. Every step is locally reasonable. After enough steps the theory agrees with every measurement then available and predicts nothing at all.

The papers are competent on purpose. If they read as though they might be right, that is the point: the surface of a scientific paper — its fluency, its confidence, its apparatus of tables and appendices and closing sentences — carries almost no information about whether its content is true. The real papers of the same period, including the wrong ones, look exactly like this. So do the right ones.

The work is not an argument that automated research is worthless, nor that human researchers are infallible. Einstein spent two and a half years doing precisely what these papers do. It is an argument about how hard it is, from the inside and at the time, to tell remarkable work from wasted work — and about what a research programme needs in order to eventually find out.

이 작업은 자동화된 연구가 무가치하다거나 인간 연구자가 틀리지 않는다는 주장이 아닙니다. 아인슈타인 본인이 2년 반 동안 정확히 이 논문들이 하는 일을 했습니다. 훌륭한 연구와 낭비된 연구가 하는 동안에는 구별되지 않는다는 것, 그리고 그 구별이 언제 어떻게 오는지에 관한 작업입니다.
